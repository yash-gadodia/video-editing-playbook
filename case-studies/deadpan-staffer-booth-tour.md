# Deadpan staffer booth tour

**Status:** validated. Cut 2026-08-01 from 13 phone clips shot the same afternoon, 65s raw in, 31.3s out. Client verdict: "great job".
**Reference:** a competitor brand's own booth-tour reel at the same trade show, ~63s.

## The premise

A junior staff member gives a completely sincere product tour in a completely flat voice, while the framing says the boss made them do it. The comedy is the gap between an excited-tour script and a dead-eyed delivery. Nothing else in the edit is doing work.

This matters because it is the cheapest good format we have found. No VO, no b-roll library, no motion graphics, no second shoot day. One person, one mic, one location, 30 minutes.

## What makes it work

1. **The hook is a confession with stakes, not a claim.** "My boss gave me a mic and told me to go viral" opens a loop about a person, not a product. The reference went further with a threat ("if this doesn't go viral you won't see me again").
2. **The prop carries the premise.** A handheld mic held like a lollipop, right under the chin, reads as evidence of being made to do this. Without it the flat delivery just reads as a bad take.
3. **Sincere information under the gag.** You genuinely learn the product range. The comedy is the delivery mechanism, not a replacement for content.
4. **One crack, late.** Exactly one beat where the deadpan breaks - in ours, a dog eating a sample: "okay, this one quite cute. Anyways." That single beat is the likeability payoff, and the immediate return to flat is what makes it funny rather than sentimental. The reference had the same shape (a genuine flash of enthusiasm about an ingredient).
5. **Sympathy CTA, not a sales CTA.** "I'm here until 8pm. Please help me" converts without selling.
6. **Visible jump cuts, no transitions.** Polish actively hurts. The format is claiming to be an unwilling amateur.

## What to fix from the reference

It ran 63s and sagged badly between 26-42s, with three shots of the presenter standing doing nothing. **30-40s is the ceiling for this format.** Cut every beat that is not either information or a joke.

## Shooting notes

- Full body for the hook and the close, bookended on the same spot. Limp arms at the side are part of the joke; a waist-up crop throws that away.
- Punch in only for product beats.
- Stop recording between every beat. No walk-and-talk. The cuts do the work.
- Use a real mic. Trade-show floor noise destroys phone audio, and the mic is the prop anyway.
- Get 2-3 takes of the hook and the close only. Everything else, one take is enough.
- Brief the talent on exactly one thing: never smile, never sell, read every line like a fire safety notice.

## Editing notes specific to this format

- **End a take one word early if the presenter breaks.** Ours said "...go viral. This is our booth" and turned away mid-"booth". Cutting on "viral" removed the break and landed the joke harder. Check the tail frames of every take, not just the word timings.
- **Order beats so a named person is mentioned immediately before their shot appears.** Moving "the founders have eaten it before" to sit directly in front of "that's the founder" turned a throwaway line into a setup.
- **Do not cut in shoot order.** Ours: hook, two product beats, how-it-is-made, the human-grade gag, the boss, the promo board, the crack, the close.
- The flatness is the sound design. Music at 7-8% or none.
